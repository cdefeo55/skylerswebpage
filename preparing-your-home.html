Preparing Your Home to Sell in Vermont.

Your home has one chance to make a strong first impression. This checklist is designed to help sellers focus on the details that influence photography, showings, buyer confidence, and the way the property is positioned when it reaches the market.

Checklist

Focus on what you can control.

Market conditions, recent sales, buyer demand, location, and competing inventory all matter. Preparation is where a seller can directly influence presentation, access, condition, and perceived value.

How to use this checklist: Start with the most visible items first: curb appeal, entry, main living spaces, kitchen, and bathrooms. Then move through storage, mechanical areas, and show-day routines. You do not need to do everything at once; the goal is to create a cleaner, more confident first impression before the home is photographed and shown.

Curb Appeal

The exterior sets expectations before a buyer steps inside. Prioritize clean, simple, well-maintained presentation.

  • Mow the lawn and trim shrubs
  • Edge gardens and walkways
  • Weed and mulch planting beds
  • Sweep walkways, driveway, porch, and entry areas
  • Remove toys, clutter, branches, and seasonal debris
  • Add fresh mulch or simple planters where appropriate
  • Clean or repair patio, deck, and railings
  • Replace old storm doors or damaged screens
  • Check exterior lighting and front door hardware
  • Touch up exterior paint where needed

Entry and Main Living Areas

Buyers should feel space, light, and calm. The goal is not to erase the home’s character, but to reduce anything that distracts from the property.

  • Remove extra furniture, worn rugs, and bulky items
  • Create clear walking paths through each room
  • Repair loose knobs, squeaky doors, and small visible issues
  • Clean windows, sills, blinds, and trim
  • Add a fresh coat of light, neutral paint where needed
  • Remove personal valuables, papers, and excess decor
  • Make rooms feel open, balanced, and easy to understand
  • Use soft lighting and open shades before showings

Kitchen

Kitchens carry a lot of weight in buyer perception. Clean surfaces, organization, and good lighting can make the room feel more valuable.

  • Make sure appliances are clean inside and out
  • Clear countertops of nonessential items
  • Organize cabinets, pantry, and drawers
  • Clean grout, sink, fixtures, backsplash, and refrigerator top
  • Repair loose handles, sticking drawers, or visible wear
  • Remove trash, pet bowls, and cleaning supplies before showings
  • Consider a simple bowl of fruit or fresh greenery
  • Make sure lighting is bright and consistent

Bathrooms

Bathrooms should feel spotless, simple, and well cared for. Small details can either build or reduce buyer confidence.

  • Remove rust, mildew, soap buildup, and old caulk
  • Clean tile, glass, mirrors, fixtures, and floors
  • Replace loose caulking or grout where needed
  • Remove personal toiletries from counters and showers
  • Use fresh towels and simple neutral accessories
  • Make lighting bright and flattering
  • Fix slow drains, running toilets, or loose fixtures
  • Keep cabinets and linen closets neat

Bedrooms, Closets, and Storage

Storage matters. Buyers often open closets, basements, garages, and utility areas to understand how the home functions day to day.

  • Organize closets and remove extra items
  • Pre-pack seasonal clothing and items you will not need
  • Make bedrooms feel calm, clean, and spacious
  • Remove oversized furniture where it crowds the room
  • Tidy basement, garage, attic, and storage rooms
  • Clean utility areas and make mechanical access easy
  • Label or organize stored items if helpful
  • Provide strong overhead light in storage areas

Garage, Basement, and Attic

These practical spaces should show that the property has been cared for, not treated as overflow space.

  • Sell, donate, or discard unnecessary items
  • Sweep garage floors and remove stains where possible
  • Organize tools, bins, and outdoor equipment
  • Make sure the garage door works properly
  • Change furnace filters if needed
  • Clean around water heater, panels, and mechanical systems
  • Make sure attic or basement access is easy
  • Check for odors, moisture, or visible maintenance concerns

Show-Ready Details

The first showing window matters. Before each showing, make the home feel bright, fresh, accessible, and easy to imagine living in.

  • Open shades and turn on lights
  • Secure jewelry, documents, cash, and valuables
  • Put away toys, laundry, dishes, and personal clutter
  • Empty trash and make sure bins are tidy
  • Add a subtle fresh scent, but avoid anything overpowering
  • Make beds and straighten pillows
  • Remove pets or arrange for them to be safely out of the way
  • Make sure buyers can access all agreed-upon areas
